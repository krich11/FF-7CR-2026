## 1. The goal and where we stand
- Playoff seeding is by record. Total points scored breaks ties (see RULES.md). Wins are the goal; points are the tiebreaker, so they still matter every week.
- As of Fri Oct 23 2026 we are 1-5 with 612.8 points. Sixth place is 3-3 (The Chosen Ones 719.0, La Lightning 711.6), two games ahead with eight games left (Weeks 7 to 14). We need to win most of our remaining games, and we are behind most teams on the tiebreaker, so we need the points too. Re-check every Tuesday.
- Because we are behind, the default is to chase a win each week: start the highest projected player unless there is real news against him, and lean toward the higher-ceiling player when we are projected to lose and two players are close.

## 2. The weekly cycle (all times CT)
- Monday night: the week ends after the Monday game. Players unlock about 2 AM CT Tuesday.
- Tuesday afternoon: the set-the-week card. Every starter, every close call, and the best free agent at K, D/ST, and backup QB.
- Waivers run overnight (about 2:00 to 4:00 AM) every day except Tuesday. Wednesday's run is the big one; free agents can be added any time after a player clears.
- Thursday evening: Thursday-game players lock at kickoff. Anything involving them (start, bench, drop, trade) must be done before then.
- Friday afternoon: final injury designations for Sunday games. Re-check every flagged starter.
- Saturday afternoon: final designations for Monday-night games.
- Sunday: the dead-man and inactives check runs at 9:53 AM. Inactives come out 90 minutes before each kickoff (about 10:30 AM for noon games, 1:35 PM for 3:05 games, 1:55 PM for 3:25 games, 5:50 PM for Sunday night, and 5:45 PM Monday for a 7:15 Monday game). Swap out any starter on an inactive list before his game locks.

## 3. Setting the lineup
- Fill all nine starting slots every week with players who have a game. A bye, OUT, or inactive starter is a guaranteed zero.
- Start the highest ESPN projection at each slot unless there is a real ESPN flag or official news. An unflagged player is playing.
- Timing trap: if a starter plays later in the week than the bench player who would replace him, the replacement locks first. Decide before the earlier kickoff. Example: Week 7, Goedert (questionable, Monday night) was benched for Pitts (Sunday noon) on Thursday so we would not be stuck with a zero.
- Matchups (agreed in the War Room, Oct 23 2026): ESPN's projection already accounts for the opposing defense, so a weak defense doesn't make a player more likely to beat his projection. It does make a big game more likely. When two players project within about a point of each other and we are behind, start the one facing the worse defense. Real edges come from news ESPN hasn't absorbed yet (a teammate's injury, a role change, missed practice). Don't double-count news or recent games the projection already includes.
- Never call any slot "only X." Every week, compare our K, D/ST, and backup QB against the best free agent.
- When a starter's QB or play-caller changes, re-check that slot the same day.

## 4. Free agents and waivers
- Free agents first. A free-agent add is instant and costs nothing. A waiver claim waits for the overnight run and costs waiver priority, but the order resets weekly to the reverse of the standings, so at 1-5 a claim costs us little.
- Add a free agent before his game kicks off; after kickoff he's frozen until the week rolls over.
- Spend waiver priority only on a player who would start for us or fills a real hole for several weeks. Don't spend it on one good week or on a player whose playoff schedule is bad.
- Before naming a drop, check that he is unlocked (RULES.md), check that the add doesn't break a position cap (TE is full at three), then drop the player with the least value for the rest of the season. Consider his bye, injury timeline, and role, not just this week's projection.
- When a starter's backup takes over a job (injury, benching), check whether the new lead player is a free agent that day and act before the rest of the league does.
- Keep D/ST and K to one each unless a two-week plan needs a second.

## 5. Kicker and defense
- Both depend on the matchup. Each week, compare ours to the best free agent. If a free agent projects 1.5 or more points higher, ask Ken that same day.
- Good D/ST matchups: a weak or turnover-prone opposing offense, a backup quarterback, a low Vegas total. Good K matchups: a team with a high implied total and a dome or good weather.

## 6. Quarterback
- Start whichever of our two QBs ESPN projects higher, unless there is news. Keep a real backup who has a starting job, so an injury or bye never leaves the slot empty.
- Through Week 7: Murray starts and McCarthy is the backup. Murray's bye is past. McCarthy is on bye in Week 8, so Murray has no backup that week.

## 7. Injured reserve
- Keep the one IR slot working. Only ESPN OUT or IR players qualify. Moving an eligible player there opens a bench spot for a free agent.
- A player in IR who loses his tag entirely makes the roster invalid until he is moved out, so check IR whenever an injured player returns. That also blocks claims, adds, and trades.

## 8. Trades
- Deadline is Wed Dec 2 2026, 11:00 AM CT.
- Look for teams with a surplus at a position where we are thin, and offer what they need. Never send a trade that strengthens this week's opponent before the games.
- Nothing is offered without Ken's command.

- Every trade must make sense for the other team too (Ken, Fri Oct 23 2026, 1:03 PM CT). Before any offer, write down why the other manager would say yes: which of their starters it upgrades, which need it fills, and what they lose. A long-shot offer is fine, but never one that looks one-sided, because a reputation for bad offers closes doors for the rest of the season.

- Every trade gets a four-sided check before it reaches Ken, done automatically without being asked (Ken, Fri Oct 23 2026, 1:12 PM CT). Write down what we get, what we lose, what the other team gets, and what they lose, then where each side has to fill in afterward (lineup holes, bye weeks, free agents they would need, the roster spot they would have to clear). Use ESPN's rest-of-season projections, not season averages, so injuries and hot streaks don't skew the comparison. Never trade a starter for a player projected only slightly better. End with one decision, yes or no, and save the write-up under trades/.
