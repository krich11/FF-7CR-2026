## The package
- Primary: Murray + Henry for Maye.
- Alternative A (if they want more): Murray + Pitts for Maye. Bigger tight end upgrade for them; costs us a better tight end.
- Alternative B (cheaper ask): McCarthy + Henry for Maye. We keep Murray as backup; they get a backup with a Week 8 bye instead of one with no bye left.
- Rejected: any package with London, Wilson, Jacobs or Goedert. Those are starters, and Maye is not worth breaking another slot.

## Four-sided check
- We get: Maye, about four points a game better than Murray at our starting QB for every remaining week but Week 11.
- We lose: Murray (starter, replaced by Maye) and Henry (bench tight end). No other starting slot changes.
- We fill: McCarthy is the backup and starts Week 11 when Maye is off; Mendoza is a free-agent fallback. Tight end stays Goedert and Pitts. Two-for-one opens a roster spot for the Andrews waiver claim with no drop. Henry's Week 11 bye leaves the roster, which slightly eases our worst bye week.
- They get: Henry starts at tight end over Helm, about four points a game better. Murray backs up Stafford and, with his bye done, covers Week 11 when Stafford and Maye are both off.
- They lose: Maye, a bench QB they are not starting. Their starting lineup gets better, not worse.
- They fill: two-for-one means one drop; Dohnte Meyers or the Lions D/ST is an easy cut.

## Why David says yes
At 0-6 they need weekly points now. They are leaving Maye on the bench and starting a tight end who scores almost nothing. This upgrades a starting slot, gives them a Week 11 quarterback they currently lack, and costs them only a bench player.

## Case FOR (Blitz)
At 1-5 we need about four extra points a week from somewhere without weakening another slot. Maye is the biggest single upgrade available at any position for a bench tight end, because Murray is replaceable: a free agent projects about the same as him. We pay with surplus, not starters.

## Strongest counterargument
Maye scored under ten in each of his first three games, and his bye lands in Week 11, our worst bye week. Murray's projection is close to the free-agent level, so the real gain depends on Maye staying near his recent pace rather than his early-season form. David may also simply keep Maye, since he just put up 27 on their bench.

## The one fact that would change the call
If Tuesday's ESPN rest-of-season projections show the gap between Maye and Murray at under two points a game, or ESPN tags Maye with any injury, the answer becomes no.
