## How the numbers work

- **Over replacement**: his clean-game average minus the average of the top 3 available players at his position (free agents plus waivers) over Weeks 1-7. Only players with at least 3 games, and not OUT or on IR, count.
- **Over projection**: his average actual minus his average ESPN weekly projection over the same clean games.
- **Trend**: average of his last 3 clean games minus his season average. Rising is +2 or more; Falling is -2 or less. Weighted avg counts the last 3 games double.
- **Upside rate**: share of clean games at or above a typical league starter at his position (12 teams: 12th QB, 24th RB/WR, 12th TE/K/D/ST by points per game, at least 3 games).
- **ROS/g**: ESPN rest-of-season projected total divided by his team's remaining games from Week 7 on.
- **Classes**: Gem = at least 3 over replacement and at or above projection. Solid = 2 or more over replacement. Replaceable = within about 2 of replacement, or steady but rarely reaching starter level. Turd = below replacement and under projection, or far below replacement.

## Data notes

- Available-player averages use every game ESPN logs as played. They are not injury-cleaned like ours, so a few may be dragged down by injury games.
- Available pool was double-checked with a separate free-agent and waiver pull of up to 150 players per position (all statuses: FREEAGENT, WAIVERS). Nobody averaging over 6 with 3+ games was missed.
- D/ST oddity: the top available D/STs (8.2) beat the 12th-best D/ST (6.0) because a couple of free-agent defenses had one huge game (Bucs 28 in Week 7, Bengals 20 in Week 1).
- ROS per game assumes ESPN's rest-of-season total still includes Week 7. ESPN hasn't rolled it forward while Week 7 is in progress, and this matches the weekly projections.
- Early ESPN projections for backups (Gordon, McCarthy) were tiny, which inflates their over-projection numbers.
- Goedert's 3 clean games and McCarthy's 3 are thin samples.
