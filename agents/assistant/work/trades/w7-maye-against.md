# The case against trading for Drake Maye (adversarial review, Week 7)

Written Sun Oct 25 2026, about 2:30 PM CT, by Assistant for Blitz and Ken. ESPN read (read-only) at about 2:18 PM CT, while Week 7 games were in progress. Research only. No offer was sent and nothing was written to ESPN. Blitz is writing the case for the trade; this file argues against it, as Ken asked. It assumes the Goff offer will not happen.

## The strongest argument against

Drake Maye is a real upgrade on paper, but no package that works for both teams fits our roster. David's Daring Team should be starting Maye, because ESPN projects him well above Matthew Stafford for the rest of the season. That means they would be giving up their best quarterback, and to accept they need a tight end who clearly beats Gunnar Helm. The only tight end we have who does that is Dallas Goedert. He is our starting tight end, Ken picked him over Pitts on Saturday, and he is our only tight end who plays in Week 11. Pitts and Henry are both on bye that week. The package that does not include a starter, Hunter Henry plus RJ Harvey, gives David's less than they would lose by giving up Maye, so it fails Ken's mutual-benefit test and would probably be declined. So we either pay with a starter and open a new hole in Week 11, our hardest game of the year, or we send an offer that should not be accepted. Maye is also on bye in Week 11, so he cannot help in that game either. And we already have two quarterbacks, Kyler Murray and J.J. McCarthy, whom ESPN projects at about 17 points a game.

## Supporting facts

### 1. Maye: projection, schedule, bye

- ESPN projects Maye for 228 points over the rest of the season. That is about 23 points a game across the ten games New England has left. He is the fourth-highest rest-of-season QB total in the league, behind Josh Allen, Joe Burrow and Jayden Daniels. ESPN shows him 98% rostered and ACTIVE with no injury tag.
- His recent scoring is strong. He scored 23.8 in Week 6 and 27.4 in Week 7 (Thursday at Chicago), and he has 138.5 points in seven games this season. Ken's rule is to compare quarterbacks on ESPN's rest-of-season projection, so these are context, not the basis.
- His bye is Week 11. His remaining regular-season schedule is at Miami in Week 8, Green Bay in Week 9, at Detroit in Week 10, the bye in Week 11, at the Chargers in Week 12, Buffalo in Week 13 and Minnesota in Week 14. In the fantasy playoffs he plays at Kansas City in Week 15, at the Jets in Week 16 and Denver in Week 17.
- So Maye can start for us in six of our seven remaining regular-season weeks, not seven.

### 2. Our quarterbacks: Murray and McCarthy

- Kyler Murray (Minnesota) projects at 202 points, about 17 a game. His bye was Week 6, so he plays every remaining week. ESPN tags him ACTIVE. He had 12.4 points in today's game at the time of the read, with the game still going. His season average has been well below his projection.
- J.J. McCarthy (Giants) projects at 193 points, also about 17 to 18 a game, and is ACTIVE. His bye is Week 8.
- These are the only two quarterbacks on our roster. On ESPN's projections, Maye is about five to six points a game better than either of them.
- If we get Maye, we still have to keep Murray or McCarthy for Week 11. Keeping both means carrying three quarterbacks, which uses a bench spot we need for Andrews and for bye-week fill-ins.

### 3. Who owns Maye and what they need

- Maye belongs to David's Daring Team (team 11). They are 0-6 with 678.0 points, and they are our Week 7 opponent right now. We do not play them again in the regular season, so helping them does not hurt us head-to-head.
- They start Matthew Stafford (Rams) at QB. ESPN projects Stafford at about 17 a game, well below Maye, so Maye is their best quarterback. If they trade Maye, Stafford is their only quarterback, and both he and Maye have a Week 11 bye.
- Their clear need is tight end. Gunnar Helm projects at about 4 points a game, and their only other tight end, Terrance Ferguson, is on IR. Their second wide receiver, Xavier Worthy, projects at about 10 a game, so a better receiver would also help them.
- Running back is not a need. Jonathan Taylor and Javonte Williams start, and Bhayshul Tuten (about 14 a game) sits on their bench. Ollie Gordon or RJ Harvey would not start for them.
- They hold the number 1 waiver priority, and we are number 2.

### 4. What each likely package costs us

- **Goedert plus Harvey (the backup plan in the Oct 23 comeback plan).** This is the only version that clearly meets their need. It costs our starting tight end. Goedert projects at about 10.3 a game and Pitts at about 9.8, so the weekly drop is small, but Goedert is our only tight end who plays in Week 11. Harvey projects at about 10.8 a game and is our third running back. In Week 11, Jacobs and Henderson are on bye, so without Harvey our second running back would be Woody Marks at about 9 a game.
- **Henry plus Harvey (the Goff package sent to Maye instead).** This does not include a starter, but Henry projects at only about 9 a game. That beats Helm by much less than Maye beats Stafford, so David's would be worse off. Under Ken's rule we should not send an offer that hurts the other side.
- **Gordon plus Meyers (the old Barkley pieces).** This fails for David's. Gordon would not start for them, and Meyers (about 10.9 a game) does not beat Worthy by enough to matter. For us, it also costs our second-best running back by projection.
- **Any version including Germie Bernard.** Bernard would help their receiver group, but he is our FLEX starter at about 13 a game.

### 5. The Barkley offer

- It is no longer pending. ESPN's pending list is empty. The offer (Meyers and Gordon for Barkley, sent Fri Oct 23 at 12:06 PM CT) shows as CANCELED on Sun Oct 25 at 12:06 PM CT, at its expiration time. It most likely expired without an answer. I cannot verify whether someone canceled it by hand.
- Meyers and Gordon are still ours, and no piece of a Maye package is tied up in another offer.

### 6. The Andrews pickup and roster spots

- ESPN now shows Mark Andrews on WAIVERS, not as a free agent. The playbook (playbook/CLAIMS.md) assumed he was a free agent we could add right away. As of today he would need a waiver claim, which processes Thursday.
- David's Daring Team has waiver priority 1 and needs a tight end more than any other team. They could claim Andrews themselves. That would also shrink their reason to want Goedert or Henry from us.
- The league allows three tight ends. We have three: Pitts, Goedert and Henry. Andrews can only join if a tight end leaves. A two-for-one Maye trade opens both the roster spot and the tight-end spot. A one-for-one trade would need a drop, and that drop would be McCarthy.
- Andrews (Baltimore) projects at about 8.6 a game, and his bye is Week 13.

### 7. Cost to our Weeks 8 to 14 lineup, byes included

- Week 8 (at The gg Team): Maye plays. This is the one week the trade fixes a real problem, because McCarthy is on bye and Murray has no backup.
- Week 9 (La Lightning): Bernard and the Steelers D/ST are on bye. Without Harvey, Meyers takes FLEX.
- Week 10 (at Lazy Yorkie): Goedert and Harvey are on bye anyway, so the trade costs nothing this week.
- Week 11 (Mitch's Badass Bunch, 4-2, our hardest game): Maye is on bye and Murray starts, so we gain nothing at QB. London, Pitts, Henry, Jacobs and Henderson are also on bye. If Goedert is gone, our only tight end is whoever we can add that week. If Harvey is gone, our second running back is Marks. This is where the trade hurts most.
- Weeks 12 to 14: Maye plays every week. Pitts replaces Goedert at a small weekly loss.
- Overall, Maye starts in six regular-season games. The QB gain is real, but in Weeks 8, 9 and 11 the trade also takes away depth we need.

## When the case against fails

- If David's Daring Team accepts a package without a starter, such as Henry plus Harvey or Henry plus Meyers, most of this case falls apart. We would gain about five to six points a game at QB in six games and give up only bench players.
- If ESPN lowers Murray's projection, or he misses time, McCarthy becomes our starter. Then Maye matters more, especially in Week 8, when McCarthy is on bye.
- If we can add Andrews or another tight end who plays in Week 11 before that week, losing Goedert costs only about half a point a week, and the Goedert-plus-Harvey version becomes reasonable.
- If Ken's must-win math needs a bigger weekly ceiling than our current roster has, the biggest one-position upgrade available may be worth a starter, even with the Week 11 cost.
