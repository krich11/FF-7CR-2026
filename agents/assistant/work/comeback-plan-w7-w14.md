# Comeback plan for Weeks 7 to 14 (Quantum Blitz, written Fri Oct 23 2026)

Written by Assistant for Ken and Blitz. League facts come from read-only ESPN pulls on Fri Oct 23 2026 at about 9:50 AM CT, the repo data files, and the nflverse 2026 weekly stats. Nothing was written to ESPN. Every trade and pickup below needs Ken's command before Blitz acts.

## 1. The win target

We need to finish 8-6, which means winning seven of our last eight games.

We are 1-5 with 612.8 points, the third-lowest total in the league. Sixth place today is The Chosen Ones at 3-3 with 719.0 points, and La Lightning sits right behind them at 3-3 with 711.6. I simulated the rest of the season many times using every team's scoring so far. In about half of those seasons the sixth seed finished at 7-7, and in most of the rest it finished at 8-6. Because we trail almost everyone in total points, a 7-7 finish loses the tiebreaker most of the time. An 8-6 finish gets us in most of the time.

Our roster as it stands scores like a team that reaches the playoffs about once in forty tries. The trades below raise our weekly projection by roughly ten points, which lifts that to about one in eight. That is still an uphill climb, so every week is a must-win and every extra point counts toward the tiebreaker as well.

We also have one real edge. We have allowed 806.6 points, the second-most in the league, so a good part of our record is bad luck in the schedule. That luck tends to even out.

## 3. The bold moves, ranked

All trade offers go out Tuesday morning, Oct 27, after the week rolls over and our players unlock (Henry and Henderson are locked now because New England played Thursday). Each one consolidates two of our depth players into one starter, the way the research says a team that needs wins should trade. Taken together they leave every Week 11 slot filled with a starter, which our current roster cannot do.

### Move 1. Trade Hunter Henry and RJ Harvey to Lazy Yorkie for Jared Goff.

QB is our biggest hole. Kyler Murray has averaged about 13 points in his three full games for Minnesota. Goff has averaged 23 points in five games for Detroit and has already had his bye. Lazy Yorkie is starting Aaron Rodgers and leaving Goff on their bench. Their running backs are Emanuel Wilson, Tyler Allgeier, and Braelon Allen, and their TE is Cade Otton, who averages about 6 points. Henry and Harvey would both start for them right away, so the offer fixes their two worst slots with players they will actually use. We play them in Week 10, and that is our easiest game even after this deal.

If they decline, the backup offer goes to David's Daring Team after Week 7 ends. We would send Dallas Goedert and RJ Harvey for Drake Maye, who has scored 26, 40, and 24 points in his last three games. Their only healthy TE averages under 3 points. Maye's Week 11 bye is the cost of that version.

### Move 2. Trade Jakobi Meyers and Ollie Gordon II to Buck up Buttercup! for Saquon Barkley.

This is the injured-star buy. Barkley missed two games with a hamstring injury, resumed practice Thursday, and is expected to play Monday night. His owner is 2-4 and already has Christian McCaffrey and Bucky Irving, so Barkley is sitting on their bench. Their real need is WR, where they are starting Jameson Williams and Jordan Addison. Meyers would start for them right away. ESPN projects Barkley for about 15 points a week the rest of the way. That makes him our best RB, and his Week 10 bye falls in our easiest game. We have no games left against this team, so the trade cannot come back to hurt us head-to-head.

### Move 3. Trade Dallas Goedert to David's Daring Team for Bhayshul Tuten.

David's Daring Team is 0-6 and starts Gunnar Helm at TE, who averages under 3 points. They also have three starting running backs in Jonathan Taylor, Javonte Williams, and Tuten, so Tuten sits on their bench. Tuten has averaged about 15 points and scored about 25 last week, and Jacksonville's bye is already behind us. Goedert would be the best TE they have had all season. We send this after Week 7 because they are this week's opponent. It also settles our TE question for good: Pitts is our TE, and he has scored 15 and 23 points in his last two games.

### Move 4. After Goff arrives, trade Kyler Murray and TreVeyon Henderson to Charlie Don't Surf for Parker Washington.

Charlie Don't Surf is 6-0 but starting Bo Nix, who averages under 12 points, with Sam Darnold and Tanner McKee behind him. Once Goff is ours, Murray is a spare QB for us and an upgrade for them. Parker Washington has averaged about 15 points for Jacksonville, ESPN projects him for about 16 a week, and his bye is done. He becomes our FLEX and covers Week 11 when London is off. Helping the league leader costs us nothing in the race for sixth. McCarthy stays as our backup QB.

### Move 5. Use our waiver priority every week and stream D/ST and K by matchup.

Our waiver priority resets to near the top every week, so we use it every Wednesday on the best player who would start for us. The trades above open three bench spots. Fill them with the Bengals D/ST for Week 8 at home against Tennessee, Wan'Dale Robinson, a Tennessee WR averaging about 9.5 points, as WR depth, and Mark Andrews, a Baltimore TE ESPN projects for about 9 points, as Pitts' Week 11 fill-in. Every Tuesday, start the D/ST whose opponent has the lowest Vegas team total, and start the kicker whose team has the highest one. Tennessee is the offense to target most weeks. The Jaguars face them in Weeks 10 and 12, and the Jaguars D/ST is on waivers now. Keep Etienne in the IR slot until Blitz names a rostered player who is IR-eligible to take his place.

## 4. How we set the lineup while trailing

We start the highest ESPN projection at every slot unless there is real news ESPN has not absorbed yet. A teammate's injury, a role change, or a missed practice counts as that kind of news.

When two players project within about a point of each other, we start the one with the bigger ceiling. That usually means the one facing the weaker defense, playing in the game with the higher Vegas total, or getting more targets downfield. When ESPN projects us to lose by ten or more, we apply the same lean to the FLEX and the second WR even when the gap is two or three points, because a normal week from us still loses that game.

When we have a QB and one of his own pass catchers, we start them together in close calls, because their big games tend to land on the same Sunday.

We still play for every point, because total points decide ties for the playoffs and we are behind on points today.
