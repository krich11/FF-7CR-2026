# Adversarial review: 2027 Draft Playbook (Assistant)
*Blitz, Mon Oct 12, 2026, about 1:40 AM CT. Every figure below was recomputed from `draft_2026_full.csv` (192 rows) with pandas/scipy, and league settings were pulled live from ESPN (`mSettings`, season 2026, read-only). The folder has no scripts or results files, only the CSV and the playbook, so "rerun the scripts" meant rebuilding each statistic from scratch.*

## Verdict: ADOPT WITH CHANGES
The descriptive numbers reproduce exactly and the league settings are correct. The two rules that matter most (no picks who are already out, and a reach cap) survive a tougher test than the one the playbook used. The position-timing rules (QB in rounds 7–10, TE at 8–10, "rounds 7–9 QBs beat rounds 4–6") rest on 4–9 players per cell over 4 weeks. That is noise presented as a finding. The bench rule doesn't fit the roster once the other rules are applied. Nothing in the playbook is tested against plain ADP or VBD drafting, and the playbook can't be run on draft day without a ranked board.

## Claims that fail verification or don't support the conclusion
1. **The draft time is wrong.** ESPN `draftSettings.date` = 1788483600000 = **Thu Sep 3, 2026, 8:00 PM CT**, not "about 9 PM CT." This is minor, but the checklist depends on the time being right.
2. **The count of players already ruled out contradicts the post-mortem.** The playbook says 11 picks had a Week 1 projection of 0, 2 of them ours, so 9 by other teams. `ADVERSARIAL.md` says the other 11 teams drafted **4** ruled-out players (8 games). Both can't be true. A Week 1 projection of 0 is not the same thing as "ruled out." The list includes Stroud at 154, Cam Ward at 187, Kamara at 192, Tucker and Travis Hunter, and some of these may have had a 0 projection for depth-chart or other reasons. The playbook uses the 11 to argue "appetite for injured players exists across the league." That conclusion isn't established. Use one definition (official status at draft time) in both documents.
3. **"Our 2 such picks cost about 67 points" can't be reproduced from this folder.** It comes from `ADVERSARIAL.md` and is valued at what nearby picks scored. The CSV gives 0.0 points each for Jacobs and Charbonnet. The "cost" depends entirely on the replacement baseline, so it should be cited as an estimate.
4. **The "next 12 picks" baseline mixes positions and uses hindsight.** The figures (−9.3, +11.2, +33.5, QBZ −168.2) reproduce exactly. The problem is the baseline: a kicker reach at pick 40 is compared with the RBs and WRs taken after it, so part of the "reach penalty" is really a position effect. It also scores each pick against players nobody could have known would beat it. I reran the test **within position**, regressing Weeks 1–4 points on log(ADP) and comparing residuals. Reaches of 15+ still underperform: **−5.8 vs +5.0** residual (n = 51 vs 59, SD about 17), and Spearman(residual, pick−ADP) = 0.36. So the direction holds, but the effect is about half the stated size, and the sample is 4 weeks of one season.
5. **"Rounds 7–9 QBs (70.0) beat rounds 4–6 (51.7)" is noise.** The cells hold n = 5 and n = 6, and rounds 1–3 (n = 6) are highest at 73.6. Nothing in the table is monotonic. The same goes for TE: rounds 4–6 n = 8, rounds 7–9 n = 4, rounds 1–3 n = 2. The playbook tags rule 4 "opinion" but then calls the result "Data." Neither rule should cite these means as evidence.
6. **The ADP correlation doesn't support rule 3.** The figures reproduce: RB −0.75, WR −0.71, TE −0.57, QB −0.51 (negative because a low ADP number is better). But an ADP that predicts a position well argues for taking that position *at ADP*. It doesn't argue for avoiding QB or TE in rounds 1–4. If anything, a weak ADP signal means the early QB and TE picks are the lottery others overpay for. That supports "don't reach," and it says nothing about position order.
7. **Possible ADP leakage, not verified.** The `adp` column is called "ESPN ADP" without saying when it was captured. If it is ESPN's current-season ADP, pulled after the draft, it includes this league's own picks and later news. Every reach statistic would then be partly circular. Record when ADP was pulled and its source, or rebuild from a pre-draft snapshot.

## Claims that hold (reproduced exactly)
- 12 teams, 16-round snake, 60 seconds per pick, keepers 0, `orderType DRAFT_START`. The live ESPN `pickOrder` ends with team 13 (QBZ) at 12th, giving picks 12/13, 36/37, and so on.
- Lineup: QB 1, RB 2, WR 2, TE 1, FLEX 1, D/ST 1, K 1, bench 7, IR 1 (17 slots). H2H points, full PPR (reception = 1.0), 4 per passing TD, −2 per interception, 0.04 per passing yard, 0.1 per rushing and receiving yard. **There is no standard-vs-PPR mixing in the 2026 points.** The `pts_2025` column was not audited for scoring basis.
- Waivers: `WAIVERS_TRADITIONAL`, no FAAB, order resets, 24-hour waivers, processed 11 AM CT on Mon and Wed–Sun. The playbook doesn't mention this, but it matters (see Missing pieces).
- Position counts by round, 10 QBs and 6 TEs by the end of round 5, first QB 16, 12th QB at pick 65, first TE 25, first K 40, first D/ST 53.
- Team reach averages: LY −57.9, DDT −31.2, QBZ −30.2, GGT −23.2, TE −0.95, LL −6.4. Achane at pick 12 (ADP 45.1), Pitts at 60 (ADP 103.7).
- The QB and TE round-band means (73.6/51.7/70.0/56.3 and 58.7/36.6/46.9/37.1) are arithmetically correct. Only the inference from them is wrong.
- The 2025 history (10 teams), the 404s for 2023–2024, and team-ID carryover were not re-verified. ESPN `previousSeasons` shows only [2025], which is consistent.

## Missing pieces
- **No baseline comparison.** A plain "best available by ESPN ADP" draft and a VBD draft (replacement = RB24/WR24 plus a flex share for 12 teams) were never simulated against the rules. Without that, there's no evidence any rule beats ADP. The one supported rule is essentially "draft at ADP."
- **No draft-day board.** The playbook needs a tiered list with ADP, a reach-cap column, bye week, injury status and the handcuff. "At or past ADP" can't be applied in 60 seconds without one.
- **Roster math doesn't close.** Rule 7 (5 RB + 5 WR) plus rule 4 (2 QB) plus rule 5 (up to 2 TE) plus rule 6 (D/ST + K in rounds 15–16) comes to 15–16 of 16 picks. That leaves no flexibility, and rounds 1–14 must hold exactly the RB/WR/QB/TE mix. With traditional waivers and full streaming, **the QB2 for byes is a wasted pick.** Drop it and use the slot for a 6th RB.
- **RB depth fixes are incomplete compared with this season's failures.** LESSONS shows the real problems were Achane (IR), Etienne (IR), Brooks (IR), Jacobs and Charbonnet dead, and the Allgeier drop leaving no healthy backup. The QBZ rows show **5 of our 16 picks are now on IR**, 4 of them RBs or our QB. "5 healthy RBs" helps. Missing is an injury-history screen for rounds 1–6 RBs and a cap of one high-risk RB in the first four picks. Achane, Etienne and Brooks all carried known risk.
- **Bye cap is untested.** "No more than 2 starters on one bye" is reasonable but needs the 2027 schedule, which isn't out yet. Say so.
- **No mention of the in-season gap.** The biggest losses in LESSONS (Gordon over Henderson, Shaheed over Meyers, a 16-man roster) were lineup and roster management, not draft problems. The playbook shouldn't present the draft as the main fix.
- **Draft slot.** ESPN randomizes the order at draft start, so slot notes are fine, but the 1–3 and 4–9 notes are generic opinion with no data behind them.

## Required changes before adoption
1. Fix the draft time to 8:00 PM CT. Reconcile the "ruled out" count with `ADVERSARIAL.md` using official status at draft time, not projection = 0.
2. Replace the reach evidence with the within-position residual test (−5.8 vs +5.0, n = 110), and state the sample: 4 weeks, one season, 192 picks.
3. Keep rule 1 (no players already out before round 14) and rule 2 (reach cap). Drop the "Data:" citations from rules 3, 4 and 5 and relabel them opinion. Change rule 3 to "rounds 1–4: best available within the reach cap; QB or TE only at or past ADP."
4. Delete the QB2 from rule 4 (we stream off traditional waivers). Make the roster math explicit: 1 QB, 6 RB, 5 WR, 2 TE, D/ST, K = 16.
5. Add an RB injury-risk screen for rounds 1–6 and require the handcuff for our RB1, not just "if available by round 11."
6. Record the ADP source and when it was pulled. Build a VBD/ADP tier board as a checked-in CSV for draft day.
7. Before August 2027, simulate the rules against plain ADP and VBD on 2026 full-season data, not Weeks 1–4. If a rule doesn't beat ADP, cut it.
